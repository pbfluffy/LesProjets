## 2. Long description ("About This Game" blocks)

Steam's "About This Game" accepts headings, bullet lists and images (GIFs are the best-converting content). Use short blocks, one idea each, and put a GIF or screenshot under every heading. Suggested order:

### Block 1: hook (top of the page, no heading)

Monsters have taken over the roads around a mountain village. Gather a party of heroes, march out, and relight the watchtowers one by one.

**Wildlight Expedition** is a cosy, painted-storybook fantasy RPG you can play in short visits or leave running in the background. Your heroes fight on their own. You decide who joins the party, what they wear, when their skills fire, and which road you tackle next.

*[GIF: a boss fight, a skill firing, the "VICTORY" banner with three stars]*

### Block 2: Eight heroes, each with their own weapons

Start as **Milo**, an ordinary farmhand with a pitchfork, and hire seven more as your gold grows.

- **Milo Dunn**, farmhand (pitchfork, wood axe, club): weak at the start, strong in your heart
- **Bren Alder**, guardian (swords, shield, rapier, saber, greatsword): holds the front line
- **Tavi Reed**, archer (bows, crossbow): fires from the back, one arrow rain at a time
- **Iona Moss**, herbalist (staves, wands): heals the party and calls lightning
- **Rook**, rogue (daggers): fast hands, many crits
- **Elric Vane**, mage (tomes, grimoires): fragile, but his fireballs and meteors hit hard
- **Maren Ashe**, lancer (spear, halberd): her spear reaches past the first monster
- **Brother Tobin**, monk (hand wraps, iron gauntlets): bare-fisted stuns and healing

Every hero has their own picture for every weapon they hold, so you see the change on screen the moment you equip it. Take up to four into a fight, rest the others in camp.

### Block 3: Armor you can see

Heroes start in a plain traveller's garb. Find **leather, chainmail and plate** and each hero's picture changes: armor is painted for every hero, in idle and attack poses. Armor trades a little damage for a lot of health, and every grade makes it stronger.

*[Screenshot: the party in plate and chainmail]*

### Block 4: Six regions, one long road

Follow the map from **Pinewood Vale** through **Mirewood**, **Frostpeak** and the **Emberwaste**, and on into the **Goblin Warrens** and the **Orc Highlands**.

- 19 stages with painted backgrounds and a hand-painted map for every region
- 30 monsters, from brushfangs and bog toads to ice giants, goblin shamans and orc brutes
- Six bosses, each with its own look and its own tell
- A Danger meter on every road tells you if your party is ready: *Easy, Fair fight, Risky, Too strong*

### Block 5: Auto-fights with skills

Fights play out on their own, so you can sit back, or you can take over.

- 16 hero skills (Whirlwind, Arrow Rain, Radiant Bloom, Fireball, Meteor Shower, Smoke Bomb, Iron Palm...), two per hero, unlocked at hero levels 3 and 10
- Cast by hand with one click or the keys 1 to 8, or leave **Auto** on and let the party decide
- Critical hits, bleeding, chilling, stuns, splash damage and armor piercing: every weapon fights differently
- **Repeat** a stage as long as you like and watch the run log fill with loot

### Block 6: Boss tactics

Bosses do not just have more health, they have habits. Learn the warning line and red lane of a charge before it lands, kill the shaman before it heals the pack, and be ready for the war cry that makes everyone faster.

- Telegraphed charges that hit the whole party
- Goblin Shamans that heal their friends, enraged berserkers, boss war cries
- Beat a region's boss to light its watchtower and open the next road

*[GIF: the red lane warning on a boss charge]*

### Block 7: Farm while you are away, up to 24 hours

Send your party to farm a stage you have cleared and close the game. When you come back, claim the gold, lumber and weapons they gathered, up to a full day's worth. A farm log tells you what each trip found.

### Block 8: Forge and merge: loot that matters

Weapons and armor cannot be bought. They drop from fights and from farming, and the stage card shows the real chance of each drop.

- Merge **3 copies** of the same item into the next grade: Common, Fine, Rare, Epic, **Legendary**
- Merge ready? A badge tells you, and **Merge all** does the whole pile at once
- A "Loot to hunt" row on every stage shows what you can win there

### Block 9: Made for a phone, comfortable on a PC

The game began on phones and keeps the big buttons and simple screens: you can learn it in a minute. On a PC you get a two-column layout, mouse hover, keyboard shortcuts (1 to 8 skills, R repeat, M mute, 1/2/3 tabs, Esc) and a window that scales to any size.

### Feature bullets (the bullet list for a "Key features" block or the capsule text)

- **8 heroes** with their own weapons and pictures: farmhand, guardian, archer, herbalist, rogue, mage, lancer and monk
- **26 weapons and 3 armor sets in 5 grades**, all visible on the heroes
- **6 regions, 19 stages, 30 monsters**, painted backgrounds and maps
- **Auto-fights with 16 hero skills**: cast them yourself or leave them on Auto
- **Boss tactics**: read the telegraph, kill the healer, survive the war cry
- **Offline farming for up to 24 hours** and a log of what you found
- **Forge merging**: 3 copies make the next grade, up to Legendary
- **Phone-friendly UI** that is just as comfortable with mouse and keyboard
- **Recorded music and sound effects** (CC0 sources, credited in the game files)

## 4. Price recommendation

**Launch price: $4.99 USD** (set the base price in Steamworks, let Steam's regional price suggestions fill the rest and check them).
**Launch discount: 15% for the first week** (about $4.24), the smallest discount that still counts; with a game this cheap the discount is a visibility nudge, not a sale.

Reasoning:
- **What a semi-idle RPG sells for.** Steam's idle and incremental games sit mostly between free and about $10. A check at the time of writing found *Melvor Idle* (a long-running idle RPG, Jagex) at **$9.99** with frequent 50% sales to **$4.99**; the low end of the category is $2.99 to $4.99 for small solo titles, and a large free-to-play group (Idle Champions, Clicker Heroes, many "Idle" mobile ports) sets the expectation that "idle" means cheap. (Prices change; look at the live pages of 5 to 10 competing games before you set yours, in a sheet, with their review counts. Search Steam for the tag *Idler* sorted by top sellers and by new releases.)
- **What your game adds.** 8 heroes, 6 regions, 19 stages, 26 weapons and a forge are well above a $2.99 clicker; but it is a first release by one person, with AI-generated art (a minority of players are hostile to that) and no Steam features such as achievements or cloud saves yet. $4.99 is a "why not" price for a player who read the page; $9.99 needs reviews first.
- **Revenue vs. fee.** Steam keeps 30% (less above $10M, which will not apply). At $4.99 you get about $3.50 per sale before taxes, so recouping the $100 Steam Direct fee takes only about 29 sales, and $1,000 of "Adjusted Gross Revenue" (when the fee comes back) about 200 sales.
- **The ladder.** Launch at **$4.99** (with 15% off, $4.24). When there are 50 or more reviews, add a free update (a new region, a hero, achievements) and consider raising to **$6.99**; Steam lets you change the price any time, and players who bought early are happy with a bigger game. Going the other way is easy too (a 30% to 50% seasonal sale), so there is no reason to start low "to be safe".
- **What not to do.** Do not launch free "to get players" without a plan (you can't charge later without anger), do not launch at $0.99 (it attracts bundle hunters and bad reviews), and do not launch at $9.99 as a first-time developer with no wishlists.
- **Review at 3 months.** If wishlists convert well but sales are low: lower the price or do a 30 to 40% sale during a Steam seasonal sale. If reviews say "worth more": raise it.

## 5. System requirements

The game is a static web game (HTML, JavaScript and WebP / MP3 files, about **45 MB** in total). The Steam version needs a small desktop wrapper (for example NW.js, Electron or Tauri; see the checklist) to run as an app; the numbers below are for that wrapper with a Chromium engine. Run it on a real low-end machine and a Steam Deck before you commit to them.

**Minimum:**
- OS: Windows 10 (64-bit)
- Processor: dual-core 2.0 GHz (Intel Core i3 / AMD equivalent)
- Memory: 4 GB RAM
- Graphics: any GPU with Direct3D 11 support (integrated graphics are fine)
- Storage: 500 MB available space (the wrapper adds about 150-250 MB to the 45 MB of game files)
- Sound Card: any
- Additional Notes: a mouse is enough; keyboard shortcuts are optional

**Recommended:**
- OS: Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit)
- Processor: quad-core 2.5 GHz
- Memory: 8 GB RAM
- Graphics: any GPU, 1080p display
- Storage: 500 MB available space

**macOS / Linux:** only list them if you build and *test* them on those systems. Do not tick them on hope. **Steam Deck:** do not claim support or "Verified"; Valve decides that after review. The game should work (keyboard and mouse input are supported, but text size and the controller are untested).

Before shipping, remove the in-game *test period tools* (Reset game data card, build label, unlock tools) and decide what happens with Google sign-in / cloud save and the "Install the game" web-app card (they are web features: hide or replace them in the Steam build; Steam Cloud can replace the Google account).

Steam requires developers to describe AI-generated content that is shipped with the game. For this game it is **pre-generated** content (made during development and shipped as fixed files); nothing is generated while the game runs.

**Answer in the Steamworks survey:**
- Does the game use pre-generated AI content? **Yes.**
- Content types: **Art** (all in-game pictures and store art). *Music: no, the music comes from CC0 sources. Sound effects: no, they come from CC0 packs. Text: written by the developer (check this one before you tick it: if any game text was drafted by an AI assistant, say so).*
- Does the game generate content with AI while it is running (live-generated content)? **No.**

**Plain statement to paste into the Steamworks text box and, shortened, on the store page:**

> The artwork in Wildlight Expedition (heroes, armor, monsters, backgrounds, maps, item icons and the store images and logo) was generated with AI image-generation tools and reviewed and selected by hand. The game's music and sound effects are not AI-generated: they come from CC0 (public domain) sources, credited in the game's `assets/music/CREDITS.md` and `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md` files. The game does not generate any content with AI while you play.

**Short version for the store page (under "About This Game", at the bottom, or the "Content Descriptors" note):**

> **AI disclosure:** All artwork was generated with AI tools and reviewed by hand. Music and sound effects are CC0 recordings, not AI. Nothing is generated while you play.

Notes:
